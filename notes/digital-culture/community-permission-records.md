# Treat community permissions as a maintained relationship

A proposed documentation workflow for contributions involving community knowledge or cultural materials. It is a design exercise, not evidence of obtained permission or a substitute for legal review.

> Retrospective study note compiled 2026-10-27. Archive dates are editorial slots; this is an unexecuted design exercise, not a historical activity claim.

## Question

How can a digital collection record the conditions under which contributed material may be displayed without treating one approval as permanent permission for every future use? This exercise focuses on a traceable workflow for requests, decisions, restrictions, and later changes. It asks how the publishing team can understand the agreed scope while avoiding unnecessary exposure of personal details. No community permission, ownership arrangement, or entitlement to publish is presumed by the proposed record.

## Design proposal

Propose a restricted permission register linked to public items through internal identifiers. Record the contributor's stated role, the material covered, the proposed uses, the decision date, conditions, and a contact route for later questions. Keep public attribution text separate from private contact details. Differentiate a request awaiting response from an affirmative decision. If approval is limited to one version or context, the publishing workflow should make that limit visible before a new derivative is released.
