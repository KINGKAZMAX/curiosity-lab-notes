# Present multiple interpretations without inventing a consensus

A proposed exhibit writing exercise that keeps interpretations attributable and comparable. It uses newly drafted fictional exhibit records and does not invent community testimony or interviews.

> Retrospective study note compiled 2026-10-27. Archive dates are editorial slots; this is an unexecuted design exercise, not a historical activity claim.

## Question

How can a digital exhibit show that an object may be interpreted differently without suggesting that all accounts are interchangeable? This exercise examines the relationship between evidence, interpretation, and the person or institution making a claim. It asks whether visitors can identify whose perspective they are reading and why two accounts differ. The goal is a legible structure for disagreement, not a fabricated balance of opinions or a claim that a community shares one view.
